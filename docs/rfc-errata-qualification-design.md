# Selected RFC errata qualification proposal

Design recorded 2026-11-15. This document does not change attribution policy v2,
permit additional copied content, authorize source traffic, or approve deployment.
The classification repair distinguishes unqualified scope from malformed evidence;
it does not establish rights to publish correction prose.

## Evidence and unresolved interpretation

[RFC 5378 section 1(a)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5378.html#section-1)
defines IETF Contributions to include communications addressed to the RFC Editor
within IETF activities. Section 4 separately treats non-IETF submissions. Treating
an IETF-stream erratum as a Contribution is a proposed interpretation, not an
explicit errata-specific grant established by this investigation.

[TLP 5 sections 2 and 3(c)](https://trustee.ietf.org/documents/trust-legal-provisions/tlp-5/)
distinguish applicable dates and permit specified unmodified reproduction with
attribution and notices. Earlier material retains its earlier terms, and section
8 addresses other streams. The corrected text has an effective date of March 25,
2015. Its September 21, 2021 correction concerns the BSD license name. The
[official version archive](https://trustee.ietf.org/documents/trust-legal-provisions/)
is the starting point for identifying earlier provisions; current terms alone
cannot qualify a historical contribution.

The [RFC Editor errata guidance](https://www.rfc-editor.org/series/rfc-errata/)
distinguishes reported, verified, rejected and held reports from the immutable
RFC. Verification status concerns technical review; it is not a licensing
decision. The [RFC usage guidance](https://www.rfc-editor.org/series/rfc-use/)
does not independently establish a blanket grant for all submitted corrections.
These official sources were checked on 2026-11-15. This investigation did not
establish explicit all-era, all-stream submission terms or inspect an individual
historical submission's agreement.

Positive qualification therefore needs an approved interpretation or explicit
applicable evidence from the RFC Editor/Trust before implementation. Public
availability, an RFC's original notice, an erratum's status, or a publisher-name
metadata notice is insufficient on its own. Unknown applicability remains an
explicit unavailable scope. No permission is inferred for the selected production
errata capture by this proposal.

## Proposed evidence boundary

Use selected errata-member subjects rather than approving the multi-decade export
as one content scope. Bind each member to its aggregate capture, exact export body
digest, errata ID, referenced RFC number and exact retained record digest. Keep
original fields and status values; do not apply corrections to RFC text.

Verify the exact historical export before extracting the selected member. A newer
export cannot establish the bytes of a retained capture. Require retained evidence
of report date, stream and contributor attribution, plus any subsequent editing
or authorship information necessary to establish the selected record's scope.
Missing or ambiguous evidence remains unavailable.

An initial applicability proposal could cover confirmed IETF-stream contributions
after March 25, 2015, using TLP 5. That date/stream filter is only a proposed review
boundary, not permission to enable it. Earlier dates and other streams require
their own reviewed evidence and recipe. In particular, RFC 9000 membership alone
does not establish every correction's submission terms.

Acquire the applicable version of the terms, evidence supporting applicability,
and the referenced RFC's exact original notice. Preserve their bytes/digests and
acquisition receipts. Cover quoted RFC text and newly contributed correction or
notes text separately. Publish complete readable contributor/source attribution,
applicable terms and original notices in an immutable member generation. Do not
invent an SPDX identifier or silently truncate notices.

Terms acquisition must use reviewed fixed operations and destinations, never
caller-supplied or discovered arbitrary notice URLs. Adding `trustee.ietf.org` to
broker/network admission would require separate Security Boundary review and
network/minikube acceptance. This repair adds no such outbound authority. Receipt
reuse must match exact bytes, applicability and the approved policy.

## Future policy, recovery and rollout

A positive recipe requires a newly registered immutable policy, proposed as epoch
3. Retain policy 1/2 compilation, verification and historical notice-resource
behavior. Because the current required epoch is global, promotion affects all
providers' readiness and must include a bounded all-provider requalification and
capacity assessment, compatibility checks and an approved rollout/rollback plan.
Do not change that global boundary as an incidental RFC fix.

Keep the blocked policy-2 target and its historical generations. A policy-3 target
has its own identity and follows ordinary fenced acquisition/publication. Any
same-epoch recovery requires a separately reviewed, versioned exact-target recipe
with expected-state and owner checks; this proposal supplies no recovery command
or batch authority. Preserve attempt state and existing immutable evidence.

Required future fixtures cover selected-member identity, changed export and wrong
member rejection, missing date/stream/authorship/terms, quoted versus contributed
text, complete notices, dependency bounds, cancellation, ownership loss, historical
recipe dispatch and unchanged old generation reads. Network tests must reject
unapproved destinations. Promotion tests must demonstrate honest readiness for
other providers. A later fixed-record live acceptance must still count unresolved
errata content as incomplete; a correctly classified error is not content success.
